I'm an electrical & computer engineer and a PhD student at Cornell University in the Napp Lab, where I work on sensing and state estimation for tiny robots. I'm also an engineer at two startups, Empathera and Clover Common Robotics.

Research

At Cornell, I work on robotics, especially sensing and state estimation for small robots that don't have much room for sensors or computing power. Before coming to Ithaca, I did research at Yale's Lens Media Lab and Western Washington University's ASPECT Lab.

Pose estimation for insect-scale robots current

state estimation · micro-aerial vehicles · embedded

Insect-sized flying robots can't carry much. I'm building a small receiver and estimator that tracks a robot's position and orientation to under a millimeter, combining IMU data with laser sweeps from off-the-shelf VR base stations, and it runs in real time on a microcontroller.

Haptic perception for robot manipulation

robot learning · haptics · datasets

I contributed to CLAMP with Cornell's EmPRISE Lab: a large crowdsourced haptic dataset, collected with an open-source device, for teaching robots what objects are made of.

Computational analysis of art materials

computer vision · machine learning · cultural heritage

At Yale's Lens Media Lab and WWU, I used imaging and neural networks to study the surfaces of photographic and printmaking papers: measuring surface roughness from raking-light images, and comparing paper textures (including in Matisse lithographs) with triplet networks.

Machine learning-based texture classification tool

web app · machine learning

A web tool from my time at Yale's IPCH Lens Media Lab for characterizing the texture of photographic papers. It used a semi-supervised triplet-loss neural network (originally developed in the ASPECT Lab at WWU) to place a paper's texture somewhere between smooth and rough, and to match researchers' uploaded samples against the lab's reference collection. Built for conservators, researchers, and historians of photography. Formerly at phototexture.net.

Tiny Tapeout: 6-bit shift register in fabrication

ASIC · Tiny Tapeout · Wokwi

My project from my first Tiny Tapeout workshop: a 6-bit shift register with toggle-enabled feedback, currently being manufactured on a real chip. It fits in a single 1×1 tile and has clock, reset, and two data inputs driving six outputs. [Wokwi design]
